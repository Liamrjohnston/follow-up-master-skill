# Edge cases

**Several people in a business deal.** Find who signs and who influences. Help your contact sell it inside: a short summary in the decider's terms, or a short call with both. Ask your contact what the decider will worry about. Don't email the boss behind your contact's back.

**No thread, only a description.** Ask for the thread once. If they can't paste it, diagnose from what they describe and say the read is weaker without their exact words.

**They already said no.** Respect it. At most one gracious line ("Thanks for letting me know. If anything changes, I'm here."), and none if they asked you to stop. No "just one more thing".

**Very old leads (months).** Treat as a fresh start. Don't pretend no time has passed or apologise for it at length. One short note with one new, useful thing, then the reset line, then stop.

**The owner has already sent three or more chasers.** One message only: it answers the reason and gives the easy way out in the same message. Then stop. Say plainly that more chasers are likely hurting.

**The owner wants something pushy.** Fake deadlines ("price goes up Friday" when it doesn't), fake scarcity, guilt, or "last chance" when it isn't. Decline in one line and give the honest version that does the job. A real deadline the owner gives you is fine to use.

**Regulated or sensitive work** (health, legal, finance). Describe what happens and what the next step is. No promised outcomes.

**Consent and spam rules.** For cold or marketing messages, rules like CASL, GDPR or CAN-SPAM may apply. Tell the owner to check their own position in one line. Not legal advice.

**The lead replied with a question.** That is not a follow-up job. Answer the question well and end with one small next step.

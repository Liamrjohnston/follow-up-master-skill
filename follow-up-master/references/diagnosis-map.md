# Diagnosis map: eleven reasons a lead goes quiet

Signals are evidence for a likely reason, not proof. Threads often show two at once: name the most likely, name the runner-up, and make message 1 test the guess.

**First check, every time:** what was the last concrete thing the buyer agreed to do? If nothing, add "never committed" to whatever else you find.

| # | Reason | Signals in the thread | The move | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Price** | Silence straight after the number. Asked for a cheaper version or a breakdown. Mentioned another quote or a budget. Friendly everywhere except the money. | Name it gently ("It looks like the number came in above what you'd planned"). Ask what the main concern is. If price is confirmed, offer a smaller or phased first step the owner really has. | An unprompted discount. It reads as desperate and teaches them to wait. |
| 2 | **Timing, genuinely not now** | They named a date, season or event. They said they were busy before the quote, not after it. | Agree one small step tied to their date ("Would it be a bad idea to pencil in 10 minutes the week of 6 Jan?"), then stay quiet until then. | Treating "let me think" as timing. With no date, it is usually indecision or status quo. |
| 3 | **Wrong decision maker** | "Let me talk to my partner / boss". "We" appears. Doubts about what someone else will accept. They forward things on. A junior title. | Help them sell it inside: a short summary written for the other person, or a short call with both. Or ask what the other person's main concern will be. | Pushing the contact to decide alone, or going over their head. |
| 4 | **Risk worry** | Asked about disruption, what could go wrong, references, guarantees. A careful practical question followed by a flat "Ok". A bad past experience. | Name the worry for them ("You might be wondering what happens if the launch slips"). Take risk off the table with a real workaround, phase, or smaller start the owner offers. | Promising outcomes you can't keep. Ignoring the question and chasing the quote. |
| 5 | **Indecision** | Asked for more options or more information. Comparing several choices. "I need to think about it". Shorter replies, longer gaps, moved or missed calls. | One clear recommendation with one reason. Fewer options, nothing new to read. Name the likely fear so they can confirm or correct it. | Another brochure. Urgency about the cost of waiting; it makes indecisive buyers freeze harder. |
| 6 | **Forgot or busy** | Warm and quick, then silent with no trigger. The last message was the owner's and easy to miss. A known busy season. | Short note that restates the one next step and brings one useful thing. Or "Is now a bad time to pick this back up?" The only reason where a light reminder is right. | A long recap or a re-pitch. |
| 7 | **Never committed** | Lots of praise, "sounds great, send me something", but no date, call, intro or deposit ever agreed. Few facts about their own situation. | Treat the lead as cooler than it feels. Ask for one small commitment, or one question about their situation. Spend less effort here. | "Following up on how keen you were". |
| 8 | **Status quo** | "We're managing OK for now". "Maybe next year". They never put a cost on the problem. The interest was the seller's idea. | One question about what happens if nothing changes, then one about what fixing it would be worth to them, so they say the value themselves. Check it's not indecision first. | Heavy urgency if they are really indecisive. |
| 9 | **Not listened to** | The owner's messages are long and about the offer. The buyer's questions went unanswered. The same ask sent twice. | Sum up their situation from their side, including the downsides, until it sounds right to them. No pitch. | Repeating what got the silence. |
| 10 | **Embarrassed** | Firm intent then sudden silence ("I'll get sign-off Friday"). A no-show. Messages seen but not answered. A customer who fell behind. Nervousness they mentioned. | Make it safe to come back with no blame. Don't mention the miss as a failure. Offer an easier way in. A no-oriented question. | Guilt, "just checking you're still keen", or counting their missed replies. |
| 11 | **A real no** | They chose someone else, asked to stop, or said yes to "have you given up?". | Stop. At most one gracious close-the-loop line, and none if they asked you to stop. | Trying again on another channel. |

## Telling look-alikes apart

- **Indecision vs status quo.** Indecision wants to act but fears choosing wrong: reduce choice and risk. Status quo doesn't feel the need: make the cost of no change felt. Opposite fixes.
- **Price vs risk.** If their last question was about money, price. If it was about disruption, what could go wrong, or how it works for them, risk, even if they never mention the number.
- **Timing vs indecision.** Timing comes with a date. "Let me think" without one is not timing.
- **Busy vs embarrassed.** Busy people reply eventually to an easy note. Repeated seen-not-replied after a miss is avoidance: more chasers make it worse.
